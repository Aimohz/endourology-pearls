1. Wavelength & Absorption Peaks

  • Ho:YAG vs. TFL

    Ho:YAG (2,100 nm): 0.31–0.40 mm optical penetration. TFL (1,940 nm): sits directly on the liquid water absorption peak (~4.5× higher absorption; 0.08 mm penetration), generating true micro-dust.

2. Pulse Duration & Retropulsion

  • Short vs. Long Pulse

    Short pulse (100–150 µs): high peak power for hard stones but high retropulsion. Long pulse (300–800 µs): gentle bubble collapse, minimal retropulsion. TFL reaches 0.025–0.05 J at up to 2,400 Hz.

3. Pulse Modulation & The Moses Effect

  • Double-Pulse Physics

    Pre-pulse creates a vapor channel ('Moses bubble'); main pulse passes unattenuated to the stone—extending effective non-contact distance to 1.0–1.5 mm, cutting retropulsion >50%, and boosting efficiency 20%–30%.

4. Fiber Core Optics & Thermal Duty Cycle

  • 150 µm Fibers

    Collimated TFL beams couple into 150 µm fibers preserving >90%–95% scope deflection and >30–40 mL/min gravity flow.

  • Thermal Safety

    Keep average power < 10 W in the ureter and ≤ 20–25 W in the kidney under standard flow; fire in 3–5 s on / 3–5 s off bursts.
