1. Metabolic Milieu & Stone Pathophysiology

  • Intestinal Solute Exchange

    Ileal/colonic reabsorption of ammonium/chloride causes hyperchloremic metabolic acidosis, driving skeletal hypercalciuria and profound hypocitraturia.

  • Mucus & Hardware Nidus

    Enteric mucus traps crystals, urease-splitting bacteria form struvite, and exposed titanium clips/staples nucleate 60%–70% of reservoir stones.

2. Preoperative Reconnaissance

  • Operative Note Mandate

    Confirm bowel segment, continence valve (Mitrofanoff/Indiana/Kock), ureteroenteric anastomosis (Bricker vs. Wallace vs. Le Duc), and afferent limb. Obtain loopogram/pouchogram + NCCT.

3. Retrograde Cannulation & Stomal Preservation

  • Ileal Conduit

    Retrograde ureteral cannulation succeeds in only 30%–60%; IV indigo carmine/fluorescein + flexible scope + angled Glidewire/Kumpe catheter aids orifice localization.

  • Continent Stoma Rule

    Never pass or torque rigid endoscopes through a continent catheterizable stoma—it destroys the continence valve. Use flexible scopes or percutaneous access.

4. Upper Tract Calculi: Antegrade Superiority

  • PCNL / ECIRS

    Antegrade percutaneous access achieves >85%–90% SFR vs. <50% retrograde, enabling through-and-through wire control via the stoma in supine or split-leg prone ECIRS.

5. Reservoir Clearance & Maintenance

  • Percutaneous vs. Open

    Puncture distended pouch away from stoma/mesentery (24–30 Fr tract + dual-action probe). Convert to mini-laparotomy/pouchotomy for massive (>4–5 cm) or staple-embedded stones.

  • Prevention

    Daily 60 mL saline/water washouts (1–2×/day) + potassium citrate (30–60 mEq/day).
