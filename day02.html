1. Renal Vascular Anatomy & The Calyceal Target

  • Avascular Plane of Brödel

    Junction of anterior and posterior renal arterial divisions positioned approximately 30° posterior to the coronal plane.

  • Papillary vs. Infundibular Entry

    Puncture strictly end-on through the posterior calyx fornix/papilla. Never puncture directly into the renal pelvis or infundibulum, where adjacent interlobar/segmental arteries risk pseudoaneurysm and AVF.

2. Puncture Geometry & Modality Selection

  • Fluoroscopic Alignment

    Bull's-Eye: C-arm rotated ~30° toward operator in calyx axis. Triangulation: bidirectional checks (0° AP for height/laterality; 30° oblique for depth).

  • Ultrasonographic Guidance

    Direct visualization of needle path avoiding retrorenal colon (~1%–2% normal, up to 10% horseshoe kidney), spleen, liver, and aberrant vessels via Color Doppler.

3. Intercostal & Supracostal Access Parameters

  • Rib Margin Rule

    Intercostal neurovascular bundles course along the inferior border of each rib—puncture strictly along the superior margin of the lower rib.

  • Supracostal Morbidity

    Above 12th rib: 8%–10% thoracic complication risk. Above 11th rib: 25%–30% risk (hydropneumothorax/hemothorax). Puncture in full expiration and obtain post-op chest imaging.

4. Tract Dilation Mechanics

  • Dilation Modalities

    High-pressure balloon (30 atm): single-step radial tamponade, preferred for routine cases. Amplatz fascial dilators: axial shear, preferred in dense retroperitoneal scarring. Alken metallic: rigid telescoping, monitor closely to prevent medial pelvic perforation.

  • Working Sheath Depth

    Sit sheath securely within the calyx/neck—never force across the UPJ or wedge into the central pelvis.

5. Acute Bleeding Management & Tamponade Protocol

  • Venous vs. Arterial Bleeding

    Low-pressure venous ooze responds to a 20–24 Fr nephrostomy tube clamped for 30–60 minutes for clot tamponade. High-pressure pulsatile bleeding around the sheath indicates arterial injury.

  • Embolization Threshold

    Persistent bleeding despite 30–60 min clamping or hemodynamic instability mandates urgent selective renal angiography and transcatheter embolization (>90% success).
