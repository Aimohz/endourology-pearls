1. Staghorn Classification & Natural History

  • Zero-Fragment Mandate

    Untreated staghorns carry a 50% renal loss rate and ~30% 10-year mortality (Blandy & Singh). Even 2–3 mm struvite residuals drive >80% regrowth within 1–2 years.

2. Primary Access Selection

  • Upper vs. Lower Pole

    Upper-pole posterior access gives a straight in-line axis to the pelvis, UPJ, and dependent calyces (8%–15% pleural risk if supracostal). Lower-pole access is reliably subcostal but limits rigid upper-pole reach.

3. Multi-Tract PCNL vs. Flexible Nephroscopy

  • Bleeding Cost

    Each additional percutaneous tract increases bleeding/transfusion risk 1.5–2×. Exhaust flexible nephroscopy and basket translocation first; if a second tract is needed, make it a 14–16 Fr mini-tract.

4. Endoscopic Combined Intrarenal Surgery (ECIRS)

  • GMSV Synergies

    Simultaneous antegrade PCNL + retrograde RIRS enables the 'pass-the-ball' maneuver, direct retrograde visualization of papillary puncture, and dual low-pressure outflow.

5. Second-Look Nephroscopy

  • Timing & Indication

    Perform at 24–72 hours via the mature tract under light sedation for residual fragments >4 mm (or any struvite residual), raising final SFR to >90%–95%.
