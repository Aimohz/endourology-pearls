Endopyelotomy & Endoureterotomy: Vascular Anatomy, Incision Mechanics & Regeneration

Campbell-Walsh Urology Ch. 88 & 93 · 2026-09-12

Selected Success
75%–85%
Length Limit
< 1.5 cm
Stent Duration
4–6 Weeks

1. Patient Selection & Success Predictors (Ch. 93)

  • Indications

    Primary robotic/laparoscopic pyeloplasty remains gold standard (>95%). Endopyelotomy achieves 75%–85% in selected cases, especially secondary UPJO (failed prior pyeloplasty).

  • Favorable Triad

    Stricture length < 1.5 cm (>2 cm fails >50%), split renal function > 20%–25%, and mild-to-moderate hydronephrosis without a crossing vessel.

2. Vascular Topography & Safe Incision Compass

  • Crossing Vessels

    Present in 30%–40% of UPJO (almost always anterior/medial); lowers endopyelotomy success to <40% and risks severe hemorrhage.

  • Incision Directions

    UPJ / Upper Ureter: strictly posterolateral. Mid-Ureter (iliac vessels): strictly anteromedial. Distal / Intramural Ureter: strictly anteromedial.

3. Incision Mechanics & The Yellow Fat Endpoint

  • Laser Settings

    Ho:YAG 365–550 µm (or 200 µm) fiber at 1.0–1.5 J, 10–15 Hz (15–20 W).

  • Mandatory Endpoint

    Incise full-thickness scar and muscularis until glistening yellow retroperitoneal fat springs open across the full stricture length. Use low-pressure gravitational irrigation to prevent urinoma.

4. Davis Intubated Ureterotomy & Stenting

  • Regeneration Biology

    Epithelial bridging occurs in 7–14 days; smooth muscle regeneration completes in 4–6 weeks.

  • Stent Sizing

    Use a tapered 7/14 Fr or 8/16 Fr endopyelotomy stent (or two parallel 6–7 Fr stents) for 4–6 weeks.

5. Postoperative Functional Surveillance

  • 3-Month MAG3 Renogram

    Obtain diuretic MAG3 scan or CTU at 3 months post-stent removal; unobstructed drainage defined as t½ < 10 minutes (10–20 min indeterminate).
