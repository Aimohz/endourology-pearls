Gray Zone: Up to ~40% of PHPT or FHH cases fall between 0.01–0.02; ~20% of PHPT have CCCR < 0.01 and ~10% of FHH exceed 0.02 (PMC10515122). Confirm ambiguous cases with CASR genetic testing.

Secondary HPT (SHPT) Management

  • Non-CKD / Vitamin D Deficiency: Replete with oral cholecalciferol (Vitamin D3) to restore 25-OH-D > 30 ng/mL (Endocrine Consensus).
  • CKD-MBD Medical Pillars: Phosphate binders, active vitamin D analogs, and calcimimetics (cinacalcet, etelcalcetide) that sensitize CaSR to suppress PTH, Ca, P, and FGF23—reducing calciphylaxis risk (StatPearls SHPT; Calciphylaxis).
  • Surgery in SHPT: Reserved as last-line for medical failure (severe bone pain, refractory hyperphosphatemia/hypercalcemia, calciphylaxis).

Tertiary HPT (THPT) & Post-Op Vigilance

  • THPT Surgical Imperative: Autonomous nodular glands rarely respond durably to medications; parathyroidectomy is recommended when no surgical contraindications exist (Frontiers Endocrinol).
  • Post-Op Hypocalcemia & Hungry Bone: Transient hypocalcemia occurs in ~50% of cases; Hungry Bone Syndrome (up to 10%) requires aggressive IV/oral calcium plus calcitriol (StatPearls).
  • Post-Op Stone Follow-Up: Repeat 24-hour urine collection; maintain urine output > 2.5 L/day, low sodium, and add potassium citrate or thiazides if hypercalciuria/hypocitraturia persists (Renal Fellow Network).
