1. Saponification & Colonic Permeability

  • Pathophysiology

    In Roux-en-Y bypass, ileal Crohn's, or chronic pancreatitis, free fatty acids bind luminal calcium (saponification), leaving oxalate unbound. Unabsorbed bile salts disrupt colonic tight junctions, increasing passive oxalate absorption 5–10× (urinary oxalate >80–150 mg/d). Requires an intact colon (end-ileostomy patients form uric acid stones instead).

2. Triple Lithogenic Defect & Oxalate Nephropathy

  • ESRD Risk

    Severe hyperoxaluria + profound hypocitraturia (<100 mg/d) + oliguria (<1.0–1.5 L/d) with low urinary calcium drives both rapid CaOx stones and silent tubulointerstitial oxalate nephropathy.

3. Targeted Medical Algorithm

  • Binders & Alkalinization

    Calcium citrate (1,000–2,000 mg/d elemental) STRICTLY WITH MEALS (preferred over carbonate due to post-bypass achlorhydria), low-fat (<40–50 g/d) + low-oxalate diet, potassium citrate (40–80 mEq/d), and cholestyramine (4 g 1–3×/d).
