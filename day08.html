1. Indications & Timing (AUA/EAU Guidelines)

  • High-Risk Workup

    Mandatory for bilateral/recurrent stones, solitary kidney, non-CaOx stones, pediatrics, PHPT, gout, dRTA, IBD, and bariatric surgery.

  • Metabolic Quiescence

    Wait 4–6 weeks after stone clearance and stent removal on habitual diet; obtain two separate 24-hour collections.

2. Creatinine Validation: Over- vs. Under-Collection

  • Expected Creatinine Index

    Men: 18–24 mg/kg/day; Women: 15–20 mg/kg/day. Consecutive 24h collections should agree within 10%–15%.

  • Over- vs. Under-Collection

    Over-collection (>25–28 mg/kg men): usually from saving Day 1's first morning void; causes false hypercalciuria/hyperoxaluria and masks hypocitraturia. Under-collection (<15–18 mg/kg men): missed voids cause false negatives and pseudo-oliguria. Check solute/Cr ratios (Ca/Cr > 0.20) or repeat before starting lifelong drugs.

3. Core 24-Hour Urine Benchmarks

  • Thresholds

    Volume ≥ 2.5 L/d (SG ≤ 1.010); Calcium > 250 mg/d (M) / > 200 mg/d (W); Oxalate < 40 mg/d; Citrate < 320 (M) / < 450 mg/d (W); Sodium < 100–150 mEq/d; pH < 5.5 (uric acid) vs. > 6.0–6.5 (dRTA/struvite).

4. Dietary Calcium Paradox & Targeted Pharmacotherapy

  • Diet

    Never restrict dietary calcium (keep 1,000–1,200 mg/d with meals to bind gut oxalate); limit nondairy animal protein to 0.8–1.0 g/kg/d.

  • Medications

    Thiazides (chlorthalidone 25–50 mg, indapamide 2.5 mg) + low sodium for hypercalciuria. Potassium citrate (30–60 mEq/d; target pH 6.2–6.8, never >7.0–7.2) for hypocitraturia/uric acid. Allopurinol (100–300 mg/d) for hyperuricosuria + normocalciuria.
