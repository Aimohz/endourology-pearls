1. Surgical Anatomy & Safe Incision Planes (Ch. 88)

  • Infundibulotomy Safety Zones

    Place incisions strictly in the superior or inferior quadrants for calyceal diverticula or infundibular stenosis. Avoid anterior incisions due to major intrarenal vascular branches.

  • Endoureterotomy Orientation

    Upper ureter / UPJ: incise laterally or posterolaterally to avoid medial/anterior crossing vessels. Distal / lower ureter: incise anteromedially away from iliac vessels.

  • Intramural Ureter Dimensions

    Narrowest ureteral segment (average luminal diameter 3–4 mm); forced dilation or unguided sheath insertion carries the highest risk of mucosal tearing or false passage.

2. Laser Lithotripsy Physics & Thermal Dynamics (Ch. 19 & 97)

  • Optical Penetration Depths

    Ho:YAG (2,100 nm): 0.31 mm in water (~4% energy at 1 mm). TFL (1,940 nm): 0.08 mm in water (~0.00024% at 1 mm).

  • Thermal Dose Risk

    TFL absorbs 4–5× more avidly in water and runs at higher frequencies; intraluminal temperatures can exceed 43 °C rapidly in closed systems. Continuous irrigation egress is mandatory.

  • Scope Protection

    Always keep flexible ureteroscopes straight when passing laser fibers to prevent working channel liner perforation and fiber fracture.

3. Intrarenal Pressure & Access Sheaths (Ch. 17 & 97)

  • IRP Control

    Baseline IRP > 30–40 cm H₂O drives pyelovenous and pyelotubular backflow, markedly increasing post-procedural bacteremia and sepsis.

  • Ureteral Access Sheaths (UAS)

    Maintains low continuous IRP and speeds retrieval, but carries up to 10%–15% risk of superficial mucosal injury and ischemic injury if oversized.

  • Golden Safety Wire Rule

    Secure guidewire placement remains the single most reliable safeguard against catastrophic loss of access, perforation, or false passage.

4. Sepsis Prevention & Antibiotic Stewardship (Ch. 16, 97 & 98)

  • Post-URS Sepsis Incidence

    Pooled rate is 5% to 6.8%. Independent predictors: indwelling pre-op stents, untreated/partially treated bacteriuria, operative time > 60 min, diabetes mellitus, and older age.

  • Stones & Recurrent UTIs

    Remove nonobstructing asymptomatic stones in recurrent UTI patients regardless of composition, and send an extracted fragment for crushed stone culture.

5. Stent Optimization & Morbidity Management (Ch. 17, 97 & 98)

  • When to Omit Stenting

    Safe only when normal baseline anatomy, uninjured ureter, non-bilateral/uncomplicated instrumentation, normal renal function, and complete stone clearance are all met.

  • Mitigating Stent Morbidity

    An α₁-adrenergic antagonist (tamsulosin 0.4 mg) combined with an NSAID significantly reduces trigonal irritation and reflux-mediated flank pain.

  • Pregnancy Stent Rule

    Exchange indwelling stents every 4 weeks in pregnant patients due to accelerated calcium/uric acid encrustation.
