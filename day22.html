1. Vascular Anatomy & Mechanisms

  • Forniceal vs. Infundibular

    Interlobar arteries hug infundibular necks; puncture end-on through the posterior papilla and avoid rigid-scope torque shearing.

2. Presentation: Immediate vs. 7–14 Day Delayed

  • Incidence & Timing

    Transfusion rate 2%–8% (standard) / 1%–2% (mini); angioembolization needed in 0.8%–1.5%. Delayed bleed classically hits at 7–14 days from clot fibrinolysis or PSA rupture.

3. Pseudoaneurysm vs. AV Fistula & Embolization

  • Differentiation

    PSA: episodic brisk hematuria + clot colic + Doppler 'yin-yang' sign. AVF: continuous hematuria, hypertension, flank machinery bruit.

  • Definitive Standard

    Avoid open exploration (30%–50% nephrectomy risk). Perform urgent DSA + superselective microcoil/Gelfoam embolization (>90%–95% first-attempt cure).
