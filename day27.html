1. The 'Acid Trap' Pathophysiology

  • pKa & Insulin Resistance

    At pH < 5.0, >85% of uric acid is insoluble (<100 mg/L). Raising pH from 5.3 to 6.5 boosts solubility >10×. Most patients have impaired proximal ammoniagenesis from type 2 diabetes/metabolic syndrome rather than hyperuricosuria.

2. Dual-Energy CT & Oral Chemolysis

  • Imaging

    Radiolucent on KUB, 300–500 HU on NCCT; Dual-Energy CT (80/140 kV) identifies uric acid with >95%–98% accuracy.

  • Medical Dissolution

    Potassium citrate 30–60 mEq/d targeting pH 6.2–6.8 dissolves >80%–90% in 4–12 weeks. Never exceed pH 7.0–7.2 (apatite crusting halts dissolution).

3. Allopurinol & Intracavitary Irrigation

  • Role of Allopurinol

    Does not dissolve existing stones; add only for hyperuricosuria (>800 M / >750 W), gout, or recurrence despite pH 6.2–6.8. Intracavitary 1/6 M NaHCO₃ or THAM (20–30 mL/h, IRP < 15–20 cm H₂O) dissolves massive staghorns in non-surgical candidates.
