1. Lower Pole Anatomy & Clearance Predictors

  • Infundibulopelvic Triad

    Acute IPA (< 30°–45°), IL > 30 mm, and narrow IW (< 4–5 mm) impede clearance and strain flexible scopes.

  • Relocation First Maneuver

    Never laser lower pole stones in situ under maximal deflection. Use a 1.3–1.9 Fr tipless nitinol basket to relocate the stone into an upper/mid calyx or renal pelvis first.

2. Active Deflection & Scope Protection

  • Fiber Sizing Impact

    365 µm fiber cuts deflection by 30%–40% and flow by >50%. A 200–242 µm (or 150 µm TFL) fiber preserves 85%–95% deflection.

  • Scope Protection Checklist

    Insert laser fiber only with scope completely straight; advance tip 1.5–2 mm past lens before deflecting or firing; strip only 2–3 mm of outer jacket.

3. Dusting vs. Fragmentation vs. Pop-Dusting

  • Dusting

    0.2–0.4 J, 40–80+ Hz (15–25 W) paintbrush etching into <1 mm dust.

  • Fragmentation & Basketing

    0.8–1.5 J, 5–10 Hz, long pulse (8–15 W) for hard stones (COM, brushite, cystine).

  • Popcorning / Pop-Dusting

    0.5–0.7 J, 20–30 Hz firing centrally in a fluid-distended calyx to pulverize 2–4 mm residual chips via vortex currents.

4. Intrarenal Thermal & Pressure Thresholds

  • Heat Dissipation

    In small calyces without egress, >20–25 W can exceed 43 °C in <10 seconds. Never fire without confirmed saline inflow and outflow.

  • Forced Irrigation Peaks

    Hand pumps or bags >150 mm Hg without a UAS can generate IRP spikes > 80–100 mm Hg.

5. Evidence-Based Exit Strategy

  • Stent Omission Criteria

    Only if pre-stented, OR time <45–60 min, complete clearance (no fragments >2 mm), no ureteral injury, normal contralateral kidney, and no infection.

  • Mandatory Stenting

    Solitary kidney, bilateral simultaneous URS, PULS ≥ 2 injury, tight UAS resistance, or baseline renal impairment.
