1. Anatomy & Pathophysiology

  • Non-Secretory Cavity

    Lined by non-secretory transitional epithelium connected via a narrow infundibular neck; stasis yields stones or 'milk of calcium' in 10%–50%. ~70% are upper-pole (often anterior).

2. Modality Selection: PCNL vs. RIRS

  • PCNL Gold Standard

    Best for posterior, mid/lower-pole, and >15–20 mm burdens (>85%–90% SFR, >80% obliteration).

  • RIRS Indication

    Preferred for anterior upper-pole diverticula, <15 mm stones, and anticoagulated patients (60%–70% SFR).

3. Retrograde vs. Percutaneous Execution

  • Retrograde Ostium Entry

    Inject dilute methylene blue/ICG to spot efflux; incise strictly in superior or inferior quadrants (avoid anterior vessels).

  • Direct PCNL & Obliteration

    Puncture directly into the diverticulum, clear stones, negotiate wire through the ostium into the pelvis, balloon-dilate (10–12 Fr), and fulgurate the mucosal lining (40–50 W Bugbee/rollerball) to obliterate the cavity.

4. Exit Strategy

  • Stenting

    Leave double-J stent 4–6 weeks across incised/dilated neck; after PCNL, leave 20–24 Fr nephrostomy tube in the fulgurated cavity 48–72 h before low-pressure nephrostogram.
